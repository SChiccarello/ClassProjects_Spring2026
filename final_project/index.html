Intro to Couponing

Couponing can be confusing, intimidating, and overwhelming...but everyone has to start somewhere, right? My goal is to simplify it just enough to spark your interest in learning more. I'm going to give you the basic knowledge so you can do more research on your own, but be able to understand what your reading. Everyone wants to save money right? But with the prices of groceries and basic necessaties today, it seems imposible! I've been couponing for over a decade, I learned from my father, and now I'm going to share my knowledge with you! Don't get discouraged if you don't understand everything the first time, it's a lot of information and it's always changing! But I promise it will be worth it when you see those savings increase and your totals decrease! Something I've started doing over the years is picking a percentage of my savings (dollar amount of coupons I've used, not sale prices) at the end of each shopping trip to put away. I save it for a rainy day fund, vacation, something special I've been wanting to buy but can't necessarily afford, etc.

History of Couponing

  1. 1887-1895 The Start: The first coupon was invented in 1887 when the owner of Coca-Cola, Asa Candler, came up with the idea to boost a new product by offering people a free drink. 1 cent off Grape Nuts cereal was the second coupon published in 1895.
  2. 1930-1940 The Great Depression & Growth: Coupons really took off to help struggling households stretch tight budgets. When supermarket chains started expanding in the 40's they used coupons to lure customers in and away from small locally owned shops.
  3. 1957 Industry Organization: The Nielsen Coupon Clearing House was established to process the growing volume of coupons and manage coupon fraud (yes, coupon fraud is a real and very common thing).
  4. 1965-1992 Peak Adoption: By 1965 half of American families were using coupons. In 1992 coupon usage peaked with 7.9 billion coupons redeemed.
  5. 1998 Culteral Milestone: National Coupon Month was established to celebrate the practice of using coupons and saving money.
  6. 2000-Present Digital Transformation: Coupons evolved from paper to online codes, store loyalty apps, and mobile coupons. In 2010 Target pioneered mobile coupons.

The Very First Coupon

More on the History of Coupons

When Asa Candler invented the first coupon for a free glass of Coca-Cola, it was to help introduce consumers to a relatively unknown beverage. The coupon was distributed through magazines and direct mail. Candlers invention played a key role in Coca-Cola's success and by 1895 Coca-Cola was being served in every state across the U.S. Within a 10 years, other companies were trying their hand at coupons. Post published their first coupon in 1895 offering 1 cent off Grape Nuts cereal. Between 1930 - 1940, during the Great Depression, the unemployment rate peaked at 25% causing households to use coupons to help stretch what little budget they had. Companies took note and began distributing coupons widely through newspapers and womens magazines. In the 1940's the risse of chain supermarkets helped popularize coupons. Stores used coupons as a way to draw customers to their stores and away from smaller locally owned grocery stores. In 1957 a company based in Iowa established Nielsen Coupon Clearing House which was the first major third party agency created to streamline the processing, sorting and redemption of manufacturer coupons for retailers. The Nielsen Coupon Clearing House allowed the coupon industry to really take off allowing retailers to send in millions of coupons to be sorted by manufacturer, validated and processed for payment. In 1974, a grocery store in Ohio scanned the first Universal Product Code, commonly known as a barcode. The invention of the barcode changed the coupon world, increasing the speed of checkout and improving coupon tracking. By the mid 1980's barcodes evolved, allowing more detailed information to be stored in them, like expiration dates. Before barcodes, coupons were processed manually by hand which was a very time consuming process and was impossible to maintain as the coupon industry grew rapidly. By th early 2000's coupons entered the digital age becoming available online allowing consumers to print coupons at home. Unfortunately this also brought to light, coupon fraud. Coupons printed online were easily manipulated and susceptible to fraud. Groups including GS1-US, Food Marketing Institute, Grocery manufacturers Association and others came together to develop a new coupon barcode that was more secure for retailers and easier for consumers to use. Over the years, evolving technology has quickly digitalized the coupon world. While we still get coupon inserts in the Sunday newspaper, it's no longer multiple inserts every week, it's honestly down to 1, maybe 2 inserts if we're lucky, a month. It's mostly the first Sunday of the month that has a coupon insert in the paper. I'll get into the new way of getting coupons a little later on, but it involves downloading different apps for the stores you're shopping at, there are still some coupons you can print online, and multiple rebate apps that allow you to cash out for online gift cards, or even into your bank account!
