Store Apps and Policies

The Digital Age of Couponing

Now a days, most stores have apps and what is referred to as digital coupons. One of the most important things to know about couponing, is the difference between a manufacturer coupon and a store coupon. As i explained in the couponing lingo list on the previous page, manufacturer coupons are ones that the store will send to the manufacturer to get reimbursed for the amount of the coupon. While store coupons are basically a discount that specific store is offering. Below I have broken down the basic steps to downloading the stores app and clipping their digital coupons. Now a very important piece of information you must remember is, just because the coupons on are that stores app, doesnt mean they are store coupons. Most, if not all of the coupons on the app will be manufacturer coupons, so as I mentioned before, make sure you are reading and understanding the fine print of each coupon. Sometimes it will say "Manufacturer Coupon" or "Store Coupon" but sometimes it isn't that obvious, which is another reason you need to familiarize yourself with each stores policy and read the fine print of each coupon. And if you still aren't sure if it's a store or manufacturers coupon, you can always ask a store associate or simply google it..."how do you tell the difference between a store and manufacturer coupon on the Food Lion app?" Something to remember also, is when you're going through the coupons, you can click on them and typically scroll down to see the exact items that qualify or "attach" to that coupon. I know I've said this a lot, but another very important thing to pay attention to when it comes to the coupon and it's fine print, is the product size and quanity. Sometimes it'll say save $1.00 off 2...which means you have to buy 2 of that item in the same transaction in order to use that coupon. You can't just buy 1 and expect to save $1.00, and they don't adjsut the coupon to match the amount of items you have. When you go to the register, scan your store loyalty card, or enter your phone number. Your digital coupons will automatically come off of your total, most commonly they will come off at the end. It's a good idea to have a total dollar amount of coupons in your mind so that you can quickly make sure they all came off before paying. If you're unsure, do not be afraid to ask the cashier to wait a moment while you double check your app. You can also always just pay, and go over your receipt before leaving the store. A lot of stores a really great about it and if for some reason a coupon doesn't come off but you believe you have the right product (flavor, size, quantity) for that coupon, you should go to customer service and talk to them. I've never gone up to customer service and have them tell me there's nothing they can do. If you believe a coupon didn't come off and you have the correct item, you will go up to customer service, pull the coupon up on your app and show them the product. They will take a look at the coupon and the fine print, if you have the correct item/quanity/sixe & flavor, they will reimburse you for the dollar amount specified on the coupon. If you do not have the correct item for whatever reason, you can always ask if you can swap it out in order to get the value of the coupon. This scenario though, is why the absolute best advice given to beginners is to start small. Do small transactions, even as an advanced couponer, sometimes I do my coupon items in a seperate transaction because it's easier to keep track of what my total should be.

Downloading and Using a Store App for Couponing

  1. In your phones app store, search for the stores you'd like to coupon (i.e Publix, FoodLion, FoodCity, Dollar General, Family Dollar, etc.) and download them. For the most part you can do the same process online if you don't want to download an app or don't have the space on your phone for one. You can always go to the stores website on your phone, tablet or laptop.
  2. After you've downloaded the app you will then create an account or login if you already have one.
  3. Once you're logged in, you will find the section of the app that has the digital coupons and start "clipping". Sometimes it's under a section labeled Extra Savings or Coupons.
  4. You can then create your shopping list right there on the app, some store apps even allow you to add coupons to your list. It's helpful to also add the coupon to your list so you can keep track of which coupons you're going to be looking to come off at the register.

Getting to Know Stores Coupon Policies

Every store has a coupon policy, and every stores policy is different. You'll be able to find the stores policy on their website or googling it. When I started couponing, I printed out the coupon policies for all the stores that I was going to be couponing at, read them a few times, highlighted important parts...like if they had double/triple days, if they had a limit on how many like coupons you could use per transaction or day, and kept them in my coupon binder so I would have them handy when planning my shopping trip and while at the store incase I hit a snag in my transaction. Sometimes, when the coupon doesn't have a limit within the fine print, the store will then put their policy in place, if they have one regarding how many like coupons you can use in a transaction and/or single day.

Key Points to Look For When Reviewing a Stores Coupon Policy

  • Stacking policy (using a manufacturer and store coupon on a single item)
  • Competitor coupons (using a coupon from a specific store at a different store than the one the coupoun is for)
  • Amount of identical coupons, amount of coupons per househould per transaction total
  • Internet printable coupons
  • Overages

Reading the entire coupon policy for each store you plan to regularly coupon at is extremely important. As I mentioned before, it's helpful to print, highlight and even make some notes on the coupon policy for each store and keep it in your coupon binder. This helps eliminate any potential issues that could come up, for example the store only allows two like coupons per household per day and you try to use four of the same coupons, even if you have 2 different types of that item, you're only allowed to use 2 of those coupons. Sometimes you can avoid something like that by breaking up transactions if the store doesn't have a limit of like coupons per day, or you're under that limit but the coupon says you're only allowed to use two like coupons in one transaction. I really can't stress enough how important it is to read and understand the fine print on a coupon and the stores coupon policy as well as keeping the stores policy readily available.
